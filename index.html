02 / The big picture

Introduction to IELTS

IELTS stands for the International English Language Testing System. It measures how effectively you can use English in real-life situations.

01 — PURPOSE

Measure proficiency

Assess your ability to understand and communicate in English, not simply memorise grammar rules.

02 — FORMAT

Four skills

Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking are tested through practical language tasks.

03 — RESULT

Band score

Each skill receives a band score from 0 to 9, along with an overall band score.

03 / Your motivation

Why is IELTS important?

An IELTS result can help you take the next step in education, work, or international experiences.

01

Study abroad

Many universities use IELTS as evidence of English-language ability for admission.

02

Career opportunities

Some employers and professional organisations require proof of English proficiency.

03

Migration pathways

IELTS is accepted by immigration authorities in several English-speaking countries, depending on the programme.

04

Personal growth

Preparing for IELTS develops confidence, communication, and independent learning skills.

04 / Choose your test

Types of IELTS

Choose the test that matches your purpose. Always check the exact requirements of your institution or organisation.

01 / ACADEMIC

IELTS Academic

Designed for people applying for higher education or professional registration where academic English is needed.

  • Academic-style reading texts
  • Describing visual information in Writing Task 1
  • Often used for university applications
02 / GENERAL TRAINING

IELTS General Training

Focuses on everyday, workplace, and social English in practical contexts.

  • Everyday and workplace reading
  • Letter writing in Writing Task 1
  • Often used for migration or work pathways
Both versions assess Listening and Speaking in the same way. Reading and Writing are different.

05 / Know the format

IELTS Test Structure

Four sections. One overall picture of your English ability.

30 minListening · 4 parts, 40 questions
60 minReading · 3 sections, 40 questions
60 minWriting · 2 tasks
11–14 minSpeaking · 3 parts, interview

Overall band score

The overall score is the average of the four skill scores, rounded to the nearest half or whole band.

Test delivery

IELTS is available in paper-based and computer-delivered formats in many locations. Availability varies by test centre.

06 / Skill focus

Listening Skills

Train your ears to recognise meaning, details, and different accents without needing to understand every single word.

01

Predict the answer

Read the questions first and predict whether the answer is a name, number, date, or place.

02

Listen for signposts

Notice phrases such as “however”, “firstly”, and “the main reason” that signal important information.

03

Practise actively

Use short recordings, answer questions, then listen again with a transcript to find missed clues.

04

Check spelling

Review plural endings, word forms, numbers, and spelling when transferring answers.

07 / Skill focus

Reading Skills

Read strategically. You do not need to translate the whole passage to find the right answer.

SKIM

Find the main idea

Read titles, topic sentences, and opening lines to understand the overall message quickly.

SCAN

Find specific details

Search for names, dates, numbers, and keywords related to the question.

VERIFY

Check the evidence

Read the surrounding sentence and identify paraphrases before choosing an answer.

Tip: practise with a timer, but focus on accuracy first. Speed improves when your strategy becomes familiar.

08 / Skill focus

Writing Skills

Clear ideas and logical organisation matter more than using complicated words in every sentence.

Task 1

Academic: Summarise and compare key features in a chart, graph, table, map, or process.

General Training: Write a letter for a specific purpose and audience.

Task 2

Write an essay that answers the question, develops relevant ideas, and supports them with explanations or examples.

  • Plan your response before writing.
  • Use clear paragraphs with one central idea each.
  • Connect ideas naturally and avoid memorised phrases.
  • Leave time to check grammar, spelling, and task response.

09 / Skill focus

Speaking Skills

The goal is natural, clear communication. You do not need a perfect accent to express ideas effectively.

PART 1

Short questions

Answer familiar questions about your home, work, studies, hobbies, and daily life.

PART 2

Long turn

Use the preparation minute to note key points, then speak on the topic for up to two minutes.

PART 3

Discussion

Explain opinions, compare ideas, discuss causes, and explore wider issues.

Daily practice: record a one-minute answer, listen for pauses and repeated words, then try again.

10 / Obstacles are normal

Common Difficulties

Knowing your weak points helps you choose the right practice instead of studying everything at once.

Limited vocabulary

Learn words in phrases and contexts; review them regularly instead of memorising long isolated lists.

Time pressure

Practise timed sections and learn when to move on from a difficult question.

Grammar mistakes

Keep an error log and focus on patterns that appear repeatedly in your writing and speaking.

Lack of confidence

Start with short, manageable speaking tasks and gradually increase the challenge.

11 / Study smarter

Effective Study Methods

A good study system is active, consistent, and based on feedback.

01 / ACTIVE RECALL

Test yourself

Close your notes and retrieve ideas, vocabulary, or answers from memory.

02 / SPACED REVIEW

Repeat over time

Review new material after increasing intervals rather than cramming once.

03 / FEEDBACK

Learn from errors

Check answers, identify why a mistake happened, and practise that exact weakness.

Try a focused 25-minute session: 20 minutes of deliberate practice + 5 minutes to review mistakes.

12 / Build your word power

Vocabulary Learning

Learn how a word works, not just what it means. Context makes vocabulary easier to remember and use.

Learn in context

  • Record a phrase or collocation.
  • Notice part of speech and word forms.
  • Write your own example sentence.

Review actively

  • Use flashcards or spaced repetition.
  • Say the word aloud and check pronunciation.
  • Try using it in speaking or writing.

15 / Make progress visible

Weekly Study Plan

A balanced routine gives every skill attention while leaving time to review and rest.

MONDAY

Listening

One practice section, transcript review, and an error log.

TUESDAY

Reading

Skimming, scanning, timed questions, and vocabulary notes.

WEDNESDAY

Writing

Plan and write one response; check structure and grammar.

THURSDAY

Speaking

Record answers, practise Part 2, and improve fluency.

FRIDAY

Vocabulary

Review saved words, collocations, and example sentences.

SATURDAY

Mock practice

Complete a timed section or combine two skills.

SUNDAY

Review & rest

Reflect on progress, set a small goal, and recharge.

REMEMBER

Consistency

Short focused sessions are better than irregular cramming.
